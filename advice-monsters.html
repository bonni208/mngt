Which Advice Monster Shows Up for You?

Before you start

This week is about coaching. Take about five minutes to look at your own advice habits and complete this snapshot.

This snapshot uses Michael Bungay Stanier's idea of advice monsters, or those voices that push us to give advice too soon. You'll see which ones show up for you in your results.

Start by thinking of one recent time someone brought you a problem, a question, or a decision. Use your current job if you can. If you aren't working right now, use a past job or a role you've put real effort into, like an internship, a volunteer role, or a team. You could also use a time a classmate, friend, roommate, or family member came to you.

    about your advice monsters

    Dr. B's note: Every one of these monsters means well. Tell-It wants to help, Save-It cares, and Control-It wants things to go right. The cost is that the other person does less of their own thinking. Working out their own answer builds their competence, and choosing their own path builds their autonomy, two important drivers of human motivation. Staying curious a little longer, before you offer advice, gives them room for both.

Why Do You Put Effort Into Your Job?

Before you start

This week is about ethics, and we start with rewards (or the degree to which we get motivated to act in certain ways, based on the incentives offered). Take a few minutes to reflect on your own reasons for putting effort into your work and complete this snapshot.

Pick one job, role, or context and keep it in mind for all 19 statements. Your current job works best. If you aren't working right now, use your most recent job or a role you've put real effort into, like an internship, a volunteer role, or a team. Wherever a statement says "job," read it as that role.

      Dr. B's note: In module 1 you looked at whether your work meets your needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. This scale looks at a different question: the reasons behind your effort. The five scores run from no reason at all, through reasons that come from outside you, to reasons you hold yourself. Most of us carry some of each, and a high score on one doesn't cancel the others. Notice which reasons are strongest for you in the job you picked, and whether they would change in a different role. Your identified regulation score connects to the values you named in Module 0, and both feed the leadership philosophy you'll draft this week.

How Well Does Your Job Meet Your Needs?

Before you start

This week is about what motivates people, starting with you. Take a few minutes to look at how well your current (or past) work meets three needs that researchers have linked to motivation and well-being, and complete this snapshot.

Pick one job, role, or context and keep it in mind for all 21 statements. Your current job works best. If you aren't working right now, use your most recent job or a role you've put real effort into, like an internship, a volunteer role, or a team. Wherever a statement says "job" or "work," read it as that role.

There's no score to aim for. This snapshot earns completion credit, and I'm interested in what you notice. Your answers stay in this browser window and aren't sent to me or saved anywhere, so be sure to take a screenshot of your name, role, and results before you exit out of this window.

From the Center for Self-Determination Theory · Basic Need Satisfaction at Work

When I Am At Work

The following questions concern your feelings about your job during the last year. (If you have been on this job for less than a year, this concerns the entire time you have been at this job.) Please indicate how true each of the following statements is for you given your experiences on this job. Remember that your boss will never know how you responded to the questions. Please use the following scale in responding to the items.

  • 1 not at all true
  • 4 somewhat true
  • 7 very true

    Deci, E. L., Ryan, R. M., Gagné, M., Leone, D. R., Usunov, J., & Kornazheva, B. P. (2001). Need satisfaction, motivation, and well-being in the work organizations of a former Eastern Bloc country. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 27(8), 930–942.

    Ilardi, B. C., Leone, D., Kasser, T., & Ryan, R. M. (1993). Employee and supervisor ratings of motivation: Main effects and discrepancies associated with job satisfaction and adjustment in a factory setting. Journal of Applied Social Psychology, 23(21), 1789–1805.

    Kasser, T., Davey, J., & Ryan, R. M. (1992). Motivation, dependability, and employee-supervisor discrepancies in psychiatric vocational rehabilitation settings. Rehabilitation Psychology, 37(3), 175–187.

    Center for Self-Determination Theory (selfdeterminationtheory.org), used for academic purposes. Adapted: grammar fix in the instructions ("statements").

    Dr. B's note: These three scores are the three basic psychological needs in self-determination theory: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. When work meets these needs, people tend to bring more energy and care to it. A lower score points to a need this job isn't meeting as well right now, and it says as much about the job as about you. Notice which need is strongest for you and which one you would most want more of. You'll see these three needs again throughout the course.

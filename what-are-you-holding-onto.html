What Are You Holding Onto?

Before you start

This week is about delegating. Take about five minutes to look at one task you keep doing yourself, what keeps you holding onto it, and what handing it off could look like, and complete this snapshot.

Pick one task you keep doing yourself that someone else could take on, now or with some help. Then think of one person who could take it. Use your current job if you can. If you aren't working right now, use a past job or a role you've put real effort into, like an internship, a volunteer role, or a team. Alternatively, you could even pick a roommate or member of your household and a task you keep hanging onto.

There's no score to aim for. This snapshot earns completion credit, and I'm interested in what you notice. I wrote these questions for this class; they're a reflection tool, not a published scale. Your answers stay in this browser window and aren't sent to me or saved anywhere, so be sure to take a screenshot of your name, task, and results before you exit out of this window.

    Dr. B's note: The reasons you checked are common, and most of them make sense in the moment. How you hand something off depends on the person: someone new to the task needs clear steps and frequent check-ins, and someone experienced needs the goal and room to run. Handing off with the right amount of support also builds the other person's competence and autonomy, two important drivers of human motivation. Handing off to get something done is managing; handing off to help someone grow is leading. Both can be effective in our strategic leadership.
